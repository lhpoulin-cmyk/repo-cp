# 2026 Mission Statement

## Build leverage.

The theme for the remainder of 2026 is **efficiency**.

Not austerity. Not doing less for the sake of doing less.

**Leverage.**

I want to get more useful capability from the intelligence, money, hardware, software, experience, time, and attention already available to me.

Before spending more, buying more, building more, or asking a more capable model to solve a problem, I want to ask:

**What do I already have, and how much more can I make it do?**

That applies to money.

It applies to hardware.

It applies to AI.

It applies to my own time.

And it applies to everything I have already learned and built.

## 1. Become dramatically more efficient

The first goal is to eliminate unnecessary expenditure without sacrificing quality.

Use deterministic software when the problem is deterministic.

Use local models when frontier intelligence is unnecessary.

Use frontier models when their additional capability creates meaningful value.

Give models grounded evidence instead of making them repeatedly rediscover facts.

Reuse mature open-source software rather than rebuilding commodity technology.

Research before implementing, but stop researching once the evidence is sufficient to act.

Build the smallest system that solves the demonstrated problem.

Measure before optimizing.

Preserve provenance.

Keep uncertainty visible.

Do not turn UNKNOWN into a convenient guess.

Efficiency should apply to the entire system:

**money, tokens, context, compute, electricity, engineering effort, maintenance, and my own attention.**

## 2. Leverage the assets I already have

The second goal is financial as much as technical.

I have already invested in computers, GPUs, networking, storage, software, education, AI services, and years of accumulated technical experience.

Those assets should compound.

A GPU should not merely be a GPU I own. It should become a qualified execution resource.

A repository should not merely contain old code. It should contain reusable engineering knowledge.

An experiment should leave evidence that makes the next experiment cheaper.

Open-source software should provide mature foundations that I can integrate rather than recreate.

Money already spent should create future capability wherever practical.

Before buying additional capacity, ask whether existing assets can satisfy the requirement.

Before paying repeatedly for computation, ask whether owned compute can perform suitable portions locally.

But financial efficiency is not simply spending less.

Spending hours of my attention to save a trivial amount of money is not efficiency.

The objective is:

> **More useful capability per dollar, per hour, per token, and per machine already owned.**

## 3. Learn to leverage frontier intelligence with consumer hardware

The third goal is the experiment underneath much of Helix.

I want to determine how much legitimate engineering capability one motivated person can develop by combining:

**frontier AI + consumer hardware + local models + mature open source + deterministic tooling + disciplined human judgment.**
